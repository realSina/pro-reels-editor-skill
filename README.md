# PRO REELS EDITOR

**Autonomous Professional Short-Form Video Editing System**

[![Version](https://img.shields.io/badge/version-1.0.0-blue.svg)](CHANGELOG.md)
[![License](https://img.shields.io/badge/license-MIT-green.svg)](LICENSE)
[![Status](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-stable-success.svg)](CHANGELOG.md)

PRO REELS EDITOR is a structured editorial skill for short-form video production.

It is designed for Reels, Shorts, TikTok, and other vertical or short-duration formats where editing decisions have to be made quickly without sacrificing clarity, pacing, or editorial consistency.

The skill focuses on the reasoning behind an edit rather than treating effects, transitions, captions, or motion graphics as the primary product.

---

## What it is

PRO REELS EDITOR defines a complete editorial workflow for turning raw footage into a finished short-form video.

It covers the process from source inspection and content understanding through editing, sound, typography, visual treatment, quality control, and final delivery.

The system is built around a simple rule:

> Every edit must earn its place.

A cut, zoom, transition, caption animation, sound effect, B-roll shot, or graphic treatment should have a reason to exist.

If a moment already works without an effect, the correct decision may be to leave it alone.

---

## Why it exists

Short-form editing often becomes overly dependent on presets and visual effects.

That can produce technically busy videos without necessarily improving the underlying piece. Excessive zooms, constant transitions, repetitive sound effects, animated captions, and arbitrary B-roll can make the edit harder to follow and eventually make every moment feel equally important.

PRO REELS EDITOR approaches the problem from the opposite direction.

The system first evaluates:

1. What is being said?
2. What is happening?
3. What is important?
4. What does the viewer need to see?
5. What is the current attention target?
6. Does the current edit communicate the moment clearly?
7. What, if anything, is missing?

Only after those questions does it decide whether an edit is necessary.

---

## Core editorial model

The decision hierarchy is intentionally ordered.

### 1. Content

The edit must preserve what actually happened and what was actually said.

The system must not invent:

* Dialogue
* Reactions
* Events
* Product behavior
* Claims
* Visual evidence
* Context that is not present in the source

### 2. Story

The sequence should make sense before it is made attractive.

A strong visual treatment cannot compensate for a confusing structure.

### 3. Comprehension

The viewer should be able to understand the current moment without fighting the edit.

This includes:

* Framing
* Timing
* Caption readability
* Visual hierarchy
* Audio clarity
* Information density

### 4. Retention

Once the content is structurally sound, pacing and attention can be optimized.

### 5. Polish

Only after the previous layers are working should decorative treatments be considered.

This includes:

* Motion
* Transitions
* SFX
* Advanced caption animation
* Color treatment
* Additional graphics

## What the skill covers

### Source analysis

Before editing, the system evaluates the available material and identifies:

* Primary footage
* Supporting footage
* B-roll
* Product shots
* Screen recordings
* Graphics
* Audio
* Music
* Existing captions
* Brand assets
* Potentially unusable material

Source material is treated according to its actual quality and relevance rather than assuming that every provided asset needs to appear in the final edit.

---

### Content understanding

The skill uses the available transcription, visual analysis, metadata, and contextual information to understand the footage.

It considers:

* Spoken content
* Speaker changes
* Pauses
* Emphasis
* Sentence boundaries
* Semantic sections
* Hooks
* Explanations
* Examples
* Punchlines
* Calls to action
* Visual events
* Supporting information

The purpose is not simply to produce a transcript.

The purpose is to understand what each section is doing in the video.

---

### Hook and opening structure

The opening is treated as a separate editorial problem.

The system evaluates:

* Initial information density
* First meaningful statement
* Visual relevance
* Unnecessary setup
* Dead air
* Delayed context
* Immediate visual opportunities

The goal is to reach the actual premise of the video without manufacturing information that was not present in the source.

---

### Semantic editing

Cuts and visual changes are tied to meaning whenever possible.

Different moments may require different treatments.

For example:

* An explanation may need clean framing.
* A key statement may benefit from subtle emphasis.
* A joke may benefit from timing or reaction coverage.
* A product demonstration may require a product-focused frame.
* A process may require B-roll or screen footage.
* A CTA may require a controlled visual emphasis.

The system does not apply a single treatment uniformly across the entire video.

---

## Attention management

At any given moment, the viewer should have a clear primary attention target.

Possible targets include:

* Speaker
* Product
* Screen
* Text
* Environment
* Reaction
* Demonstration
* Graphic

The edit should avoid competing attention targets unless the competition is intentional and useful.

This principle affects:

* Framing
* Captions
* B-roll
* Motion
* Graphics
* Sound effects
* Transitions

---

## Effect budget

Visual effects are treated as a limited resource.

The skill maintains an internal sense of:

* Effect frequency
* Recent effect usage
* Visual intensity
* Repetition
* Cooldowns
* Current attention density

The same treatment should not appear repeatedly simply because it worked once.

An effect that is useful at one point can become distracting when repeated too frequently.

`NO EFFECT` is therefore a valid editorial decision.

---

## Anti-repetition system

The skill actively checks for repetitive treatment.

This applies to:

* Zooms
* Punch-ins
* Transitions
* SFX
* Caption animations
* B-roll patterns
* Motion graphics
* Camera reframing
* Emphasis styles

Variation should not be introduced for its own sake.

The objective is controlled consistency rather than random visual variety.

---

## Face and speaker editing

For talking-head footage, the system evaluates:

* Speaker visibility
* Facial expression
* Head position
* Eye line
* Framing
* Shot changes
* Crop safety
* Relevant reactions
* Natural movement

Punch-ins and reframing should support the content rather than become a constant visual rhythm.

---

## B-roll decisions

B-roll is selected according to semantic relevance.

It may be used to:

* Explain what is being discussed
* Show a referenced object
* Demonstrate a process
* Cover a useful cut
* Introduce visual variation
* Reinforce context

B-roll should not contradict the spoken content or imply evidence that the source does not provide.

---

## Product and screen content

When the video concerns a product, application, website, interface, or process, the relevant object becomes an explicit editorial target.

The system considers:

* Visibility
* Scale
* Readability
* Cursor or interaction visibility
* UI density
* Highlighting
* Crop selection
* Screen transitions
* Demonstration timing

The visual treatment should make the relevant information easier to understand rather than simply making the screen more animated.

---

## Captions and typography

Captions are treated as part of the editorial system rather than an independent decoration layer.

The skill considers:

* Reading speed
* Line length
* Word emphasis
* Timing
* Hierarchy
* Safe areas
* Screen size
* Contrast
* Background conditions
* Language direction

Caption animation should support emphasis and pacing without making the text harder to read.

---

## Audio

The audio workflow covers:

* Speech clarity
* Noise reduction
* Level consistency
* Dialogue priority
* Music balance
* SFX placement
* Silence and pause handling
* Transitions
* Final loudness considerations

Sound effects should reinforce an editorial event rather than simply fill empty space.

---

## Music

Music is selected and treated according to:

* Content tone
* Speech density
* Energy
* Scene changes
* Brand identity
* Emotional direction
* Licensing availability

Dialogue remains the primary information source when speech is present.

---

## Color

Color treatment is intended to improve consistency and presentation without destroying source characteristics.

The workflow considers:

* Exposure
* White balance
* Contrast
* Skin tones
* Shot consistency
* Brand treatment
* Platform presentation

Heavy grading is not automatically preferable to a clean correction.

---

## Brand consistency

The system can maintain recurring visual decisions across a project, including:

* Caption treatment
* Typography
* Colors
* Logo usage
* Motion language
* SFX language
* Framing conventions
* CTA treatment
* General editing intensity

Consistency is treated as a system rather than a collection of unrelated presets.

---

## Multilingual and RTL support

The skill accounts for languages that require different typographic and layout behavior.

Particular attention is given to:

* Persian
* Arabic
* English
* Right-to-left layouts
* Mixed RTL/LTR text
* Numerals
* Punctuation
* Line breaking
* Caption alignment
* Safe areas

Text should remain readable and visually intentional regardless of language direction.

---

## Accessibility

The editing process considers viewers who may:

* Watch without sound
* Watch on small screens
* Have limited hearing
* Have limited visual attention
* Encounter captions under poor viewing conditions

Important information should not depend entirely on audio when the format requires otherwise.

---

## Source integrity

The skill does not treat visual editing as permission to fabricate evidence.

It must not:

* Invent statements
* Change the meaning of speech
* Create misleading reactions
* Use unrelated footage as if it were evidence
* Misrepresent a product
* Attribute unsupported claims to a speaker
* Present generated material as original source footage without appropriate context

Editing can change presentation.

It should not silently change the underlying meaning.

---

## Licensing and sensitive information

The workflow includes checks for:

* Third-party media
* Music licensing
* Brand assets
* Watermarks
* Copyright-sensitive material
* Personal information
* Private information
* Credentials
* Addresses
* Phone numbers
* Emails
* Sensitive UI data

Material that should not appear in the final video should be identified before delivery.

---

## Capability-aware execution

The skill is designed to operate within the capabilities actually available to the editing environment.

If a required capability is unavailable, the system should not pretend that the operation was performed.

Examples include:

* No transcription engine
* No face tracking
* No object tracking
* No video renderer
* No audio processing
* No frame extraction
* No external media search

The system should adapt its decision process to the tools it actually has.

---

## Editing passes

The workflow is divided into separate passes so that structural decisions are not mixed with finishing work.

### Pass 1 — Ingest

Inspect the available assets.

### Pass 2 — Understand

Transcribe, segment, identify speakers, and understand the content.

### Pass 3 — Structure

Build the story and remove unnecessary material.

### Pass 4 — Rhythm

Refine cuts, pauses, pacing, and attention.

### Pass 5 — Visual

Handle framing, B-roll, graphics, motion, and captions.

### Pass 6 — Audio

Clean speech and build the sound layer.

### Pass 7 — Color

Correct and match the footage.

### Pass 8 — Polish

Apply controlled finishing treatments.

### Pass 9 — QA

Review the final result from multiple viewing conditions.

---

## Quality control

Before delivery, the final edit should be reviewed for:

* Story clarity
* Factual integrity
* Caption accuracy
* Caption timing
* Readability
* Audio clarity
* Music balance
* Visual consistency
* Repetition
* Unnecessary effects
* Cropping errors
* Tracking errors
* Graphic collisions
* Safe-area violations
* Sensitive information
* Export integrity

The final review is not limited to checking whether the render completed successfully.

A technically valid render can still be a poor edit.

---

## Platform delivery

The editorial system supports short-form delivery requirements across platforms such as:

* Instagram Reels
* YouTube Shorts
* TikTok

The final output should be adapted to the intended platform while preserving the editorial decisions that define the piece.

Platform formatting is treated as a delivery concern, not a replacement for editorial judgment.

## Getting started

There is no application to install.

PRO REELS EDITOR is distributed as a standalone Markdown skill.

To use it, provide the contents of:

[`skill/PRO_REELS_EDITOR.md`](skill/PRO_REELS_EDITOR.md)

to the editing agent or AI environment that will perform the work.

The skill defines the editorial behavior. Actual execution depends on the tools available in the environment.
